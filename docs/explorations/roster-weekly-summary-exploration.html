Colour check — answered before drawing V3

#ff7a3d is real, and it is retired. It was the original brand orange, defined as --orange in reps.html — the pre-Next.js standalone prototype, which has exactly one commit to its name and is a frozen artifact. It was deliberately replaced by sky blue #378add on 2026-07-13 in commit 3ca8324, whose message names the change outright: “sky-blue theme”.

Today it exists in one tracked file — reps.html — and nowhere else. Not in src/, not in globals.css, not in tailwind.config.ts.

The trap you half-spotted: the tokens are still named --reps-orange and --reps-orange-hi, but they hold #378add and #4a9ae8 — sky blue. The name is a leftover from the rename; every “orange” button in the app renders blue. So the established brand accent is blue, and blue is already spoken for on this screen (recent-activity timestamps).

What that means for V3: it fails your test twice over — #ff7a3d is not currently established, and the colour that is established is already used here. So V3 below is not “apply the brand accent”; it is revive a killed colour, which is a decision rather than a lookup. I drew it anyway so you can judge it, using #d47a54 — #ff7a3d desaturated and darkened by the same kind of move that produced --reps-green-muted from --reps-green. Nothing was invented: the hue is the retired one, unchanged.

The tint is on the figures only. Qualifiers stay #7d8494, the cell hairline stays #2a2d36 — so the colour marks the numbers and nothing else.

It does solve a real problem. In V1 the strip is white type sitting above a white heading; the tint is what makes it read as a different kind of thing rather than as a smaller title. That is a genuine argument for it, separate from whether the hue is allowed.

Adopting it reverses a shipped decision. Orange was removed from the whole product on Jul 13 in favour of sky blue. Bringing it back for one strip means the app has an accent it uses in exactly one place, and a fourth colour meaning on a screen that currently runs three (green = Done, blue = recent, grey = idle).

It also warms toward the killed yellow. Yellow was removed platform-wide in July; at 13px on a phone, coral against #f0b429's memory is closer than the hex suggests.

Reading the three

All three are geometrically identical, measured: the strip's ink is 23.5px tall, the slot occupies 81.5px once A1's 24/34 padding is counted, and the sticky unit is 174.5px — the same three numbers in every frame. The 16px icons sit inside the existing line box without growing it, and the tint costs nothing at all. So this is purely a question of register, with no layout consequence to weigh against it.

V1 is the safest and possibly the weakest. It adds no vocabulary at all, but it leaves the strip as white type above a white heading — distinguished by spacing and nothing else. If A1's separation reads well on device, V1 is enough; if the strip still feels absorbed, that is evidence the problem was never only spacing.

V2's real cost is not the glyphs, it is the precedent. Every icon in the app today lives in an overflow menu and labels an action. These label a measure, which is a new job. And flame carries the streak convention wholesale — the gamified register the product has otherwise stayed out of. activity has no such problem.

V3 is the one with a decision attached. It is the most effective at making the strip read as its own kind of thing — but the hue was deliberately retired on Jul 13, and adopting it here gives the app an accent used in exactly one place and a fourth colour meaning on a screen that runs three. See the box at the top of this page before treating it as available.

Unchanged and still open: 7 days in a row needs defining (what counts as a day, does today count before you have assigned, midnight or rolling 24h — all in LA calendar dates); the strip needs seeing at the bottom of the range, where a new coach reads 1 day in a row and 1 of 3 active; and whatever sits in this slot is pinned by sticky top-0 over the whole list, which moving it below the heading row would avoid.
